Charts

Expanded Chart View

Tap the chart in the Technicals tab to enter full-screen immersive mode. The expanded chart fills the screen with 6 months of daily candlestick data and six overlays — each explained in detail below.

📊 Bollinger Bands

Three lines plotted around price: a 20-day Simple Moving Average (middle band) with upper and lower bands at ±2 standard deviations. The bands dynamically widen in high volatility and contract in low volatility periods.

Middle Band = SMA(20)
Upper Band  = SMA(20) + (2 × σ₂₀)
Lower Band  = SMA(20) − (2 × σ₂₀)
// σ₂₀ = standard deviation of close prices over 20 days
%B = (Price − Lower Band) / (Upper Band − Lower Band)
// %B: 0 = at lower band, 1 = at upper band, 0.5 = midline
  • ›Price touches upper band — overbought zone; ~5% of closes exceed this level statistically. Could signal reversal or momentum continuation.
  • ›Price touches lower band — oversold zone; potential bounce or continued breakdown if selling pressure persists.
  • ›Bands narrowing (squeeze) — volatility is compressing; a large directional move is building. The narrower the bands, the bigger the expected breakout.
  • ›Bands expanding rapidly — volatility explosion in progress; the trend is real and accelerating.
  • ›Walking the band — in strong trends, price "walks" along the upper (uptrend) or lower (downtrend) band for extended periods without reversing.
Signal logic: SignalForge fires a BUY when price closes near the lower band AND RSI is below 35. A SELL fires when price closes near the upper band AND RSI is above 65. Both conditions must align.
📈 EMA 12 / EMA 26

Two exponential moving averages — EMA weights recent prices more heavily than a simple average, making it faster to react to price changes. EMA-12 is the "fast" line; EMA-26 is the "slow" line.

EMA(today) = Price(today) × k + EMA(yesterday) × (1 − k)
// k (smoothing factor) = 2 ÷ (period + 1)
EMA-12: k = 0.1538    EMA-26: k = 0.0741

MACD Line = EMA-12 − EMA-26
// Positive MACD = short-term momentum is above long-term (bullish)
  • ›EMA-12 crosses above EMA-26 — bullish crossover; short-term momentum exceeding long-term. Often called a "Golden Cross" in the short-period context.
  • ›EMA-12 crosses below EMA-26 — bearish crossover; downtrend accelerating. "Death Cross" equivalent.
  • ›Price above both EMAs — bullish trend intact; EMAs act as dynamic support levels. Dips to the EMA lines are buy opportunities in uptrends.
  • ›Price below both EMAs — bearish trend; EMAs act as overhead resistance.
  • ›Spread between EMAs — the wider the gap between EMA-12 and EMA-26, the stronger the trend momentum. A converging spread signals trend exhaustion.
🔴 SuperTrend

An ATR-based trailing stop line that rides below price in an uptrend (green) and above price in a downtrend (red). It flips sides when price closes through it — creating clean buy and sell signals.

ATR(10) = Average True Range over 10 periods
// True Range = max(High−Low, |High−PrevClose|, |Low−PrevClose|)

Basic Upper = (High + Low) / 2 + (3 × ATR)
Basic Lower = (High + Low) / 2 − (3 × ATR)

SuperTrend = Basic Lower (bullish) or Basic Upper (bearish)
// Multiplier of 3 × ATR is the default — wider = fewer signals, less noise
  • ›Green line below price — uptrend confirmed; line is a rising trailing stop that locks in gains as the stock climbs.
  • ›Red line above price — downtrend confirmed; price is capped by a falling resistance ceiling.
  • ›Flip from red → green — buy signal; price closed above the descending resistance line. Often marks the start of a sustained rally.
  • ›Flip from green → red — sell signal; price closed below the ascending support line. Consider exiting or hedging.
Best conditions: SuperTrend performs well in trending markets. In choppy, sideways markets it can whipsaw repeatedly — always confirm with Bollinger Band signals or RSI before acting on a flip alone.
🌊 Keltner Channels

ATR-based envelope channels centered on an EMA. Unlike Bollinger Bands (which use standard deviation), Keltner uses Average True Range — making them less reactive to volatility spikes. The key relationship is between Bollinger Bands and Keltner Channels together.

Middle Line  = EMA(20)
Upper Channel = EMA(20) + (1.5 × ATR(10))
Lower Channel = EMA(20) − (1.5 × ATR(10))

// Squeeze condition (Bollinger Band Squeeze):
BB Upper < KC Upper AND BB Lower > KC Lower
// = Bollinger Bands are entirely inside the Keltner Channels
  • ›BB inside KC (squeeze active) — volatility has compressed below "normal" ATR levels. The market is coiling. Watch for the direction of the first breakout bar.
  • ›BB expands outside KC (squeeze fired) — volatility has been released. The move is real. Trade the direction of the breakout with a tight stop.
  • ›Price at outer Keltner lines — extreme move relative to recent ATR. Often mean-reverts; watch for exhaustion candles (doji, hammer, shooting star).
  • ›Long squeeze setups often lead to 5–15% moves once fired. The longer the squeeze, the larger the explosion tends to be.
Explorer filter: The "BB Squeeze" preset in Research → Explorer automatically scans for stocks where this condition is active right now.
📊 Volume Bars

Daily trading volume displayed as a histogram below the price chart. Green bars = up days (price closed above open). Red bars = down days. Volume is the "conviction meter" — it validates or questions every price move.

  • ›High volume on breakout (green bar) — institutional buyers are committed; the move is real. High probability of continuation.
  • ›Low volume on breakout — suspect move; retail-driven or short-squeeze. May fail and retrace (bull trap risk).
  • ›Declining volume during downtrend — selling pressure weakening; exhaustion approaching. Potential reversal setup.
  • ›Massive red spike (capitulation) — panic selling at max fear. Often marks a price bottom as sellers are exhausted. Look for a reversal candle the following session.
  • ›Steady rising volume in uptrend — accumulation; institutional buyers are building positions. The rally has legs.

Advanced Options Analytics

IV/HV Analysis & Probability Engine

IV vs HV Chart: Dual-line chart comparing implied volatility (from options) against historical/realized volatility (from actual price movement). When IV > HV, options are "expensive" — favor selling strategies. When IV < HV, options are "cheap" — favor buying.

Probability Distribution: Two models visualized side by side:

  • Lognormal — Classical bell-curve based on current price, IV, and time to expiration. Shows the theoretical probability of each future price.
  • Breeden-Litzenberger — Extracts the market's actual probability distribution from observed option prices. Captures skew and fat tails that the lognormal model misses.

The expected move zone (1 standard deviation, 68% probability) is highlighted on both distributions.

Option Greeks — Full Reference

Greeks Explained

Greeks measure how sensitive an option's price is to various market factors. SignalForge plots all four first-order Greeks across every available strike in the butterfly charts, so you can see the complete risk landscape for any expiration at a glance.

Δ Delta — Price Sensitivity

How much an option's price moves for every $1 move in the underlying stock. Delta is the most important Greek for understanding directional exposure.

Range: 0 to +1.0 for calls  |  −1.0 to 0 for puts
Delta 0.50 → option gains/loses $0.50 per $1 stock move
// Delta also ≈ probability the option expires in-the-money (ITM)
// Delta 0.30 ≈ 30% chance of expiring ITM
DeltaMoneynessWhat It Means
0.75 – 1.00Deep ITM CallMoves almost like owning 75–100 shares. High intrinsic value, low time premium. Acts like stock.
0.45 – 0.55ATM CallAt-the-money — most sensitive to price changes. 50/50 ITM probability. Highest gamma and theta.
0.10 – 0.30OTM CallCheap, speculative. Low probability. Best for defined-risk directional bets with small capital.
−0.20 to −0.30OTM Put (CSP)The income-selling sweet spot. ~70–80% probability of expiring worthless. Strong premium with manageable assignment risk.
Covered Call targeting: Sell the 0.25–0.35 delta call to balance income collection vs. probability of having shares called away. Higher delta = more premium, higher assignment risk.
Γ Gamma — Delta's Rate of Change

How quickly delta changes as the stock moves $1. Gamma is the "acceleration" of your position. It is always positive for option buyers (helps you) and always negative for option sellers (hurts you).

If Delta = 0.50 and Gamma = 0.05:
  Stock +$1 → new Delta = 0.55 (position getting longer)
  Stock −$1 → new Delta = 0.45 (position getting shorter)

// Gamma is HIGHEST for ATM options near expiration
// Gamma is LOWEST for far OTM/ITM options or long DTE
  • ›High gamma (ATM, <7 DTE) — small moves in the stock cause large swings in option value. A 1% stock move might move the option 20–30%.
  • ›Low gamma (deep ITM/OTM, long DTE) — delta changes slowly; more predictable and manageable exposure.
  • ›Gamma squeeze risk (sellers) — if a short option moves ATM near expiry, gamma accelerates losses exponentially with every tick against you.
  • ›Gamma scalping (buyers) — long gamma positions profit from big moves in either direction. The stock just has to move enough to offset theta decay.
Seller's rule: Never hold a short ATM option through expiration week. Gamma spikes dramatically in the final 5 trading days — a 2% stock move can wipe out the entire premium collected.
Θ Theta — Time Decay

How much value an option loses each calendar day from the passage of time alone, with the stock price and IV unchanged. Theta is always negative for option buyers and always positive for sellers.

Theta = −$0.05 per day per share
→ 1 contract (100 shares) loses $5.00 per day
→ 30 DTE option loses $150 over its remaining life (if linear)

// Theta decay is NOT linear — it accelerates as expiry approaches
// Roughly: ~50% of an option's time value decays in the final 30 days
  • ›For sellers (CSP, Covered Call) — theta works for you every single day. Even on flat days with no price movement, you're profiting from time decay.
  • ›For buyers (Long Call, Long Put) — the clock is always ticking against you. Your position must move fast enough to overcome daily theta bleed.
  • ›30–45 DTE sweet spot — the standard income-selling target range. Enough premium to be worthwhile, but theta decay is accelerating. Exit at 50% profit to capture most of the decay without holding through gamma risk.
  • ›Weekend effect — theta accounts for 3 days over weekends (Fri/Sat/Sun) but options only trade 5 days a week. Selling on Friday captures 3 days of decay by Monday open.
V Vega — Implied Volatility Sensitivity

How much an option's price changes for every 1 percentage point move in Implied Volatility (IV). Vega is always positive for both calls and puts — higher IV always makes options more expensive, regardless of direction.

Vega = $0.12 → option gains $0.12 per 1% rise in IV
           option loses $0.12 per 1% drop in IV

// Vega is HIGHEST for ATM options with lots of time remaining
// Vega FALLS as expiration approaches (time = vega's fuel)
  • ›IV crush after earnings — IV spikes before earnings (uncertainty premium), then collapses 30–50% after the announcement regardless of the move. Option buyers can be correct on direction and still lose money due to vega collapse.
  • ›High IV environment — options are "expensive" relative to realized moves. Favor selling strategies (CSP, Covered Call, Iron Condor) to collect rich premium.
  • ›Low IV environment — options are "cheap." Favor buying strategies (Long Call, Long Put) since you own vega and benefit when IV eventually expands.
  • ›IV vs HV check — use the IV/HV chart in SignalForge. If IV is significantly above Historical Volatility (HV), options are overpriced. If IV is below HV, they're underpriced.
IV Rank (IVR) rule of thumb: IVR > 50 = options expensive, prefer selling. IVR < 30 = options cheap, prefer buying. IVR is IV's percentile rank over the past 52 weeks.

Intrinsic ValueCalls: max(Stock − Strike, 0) | Puts: max(Strike − Stock, 0)The guaranteed value if exercised immediately. Deep ITM options are mostly intrinsic. OTM options have zero intrinsic — all time premium (extrinsic) value.
Extrinsic ValueOption Price − Intrinsic ValueThe "hope premium" — what you pay for time and volatility. All extrinsic value decays to zero at expiration. Option sellers profit from collecting and holding extrinsic value.
Technical Indicators Reference

RSI, Momentum Score & Convergence

💪 RSI — Relative Strength Index

Measures the speed and magnitude of recent price changes to identify overbought and oversold conditions. RSI oscillates between 0 and 100.

RSI = 100 − [100 / (1 + RS)]
RS = Average Gain (14 periods) / Average Loss (14 periods)

// Period: 14 days (default). SignalForge uses 14-period daily RSI.
// Gains and losses are averaged using Wilder's smoothing method
RSI LevelZoneInterpretation
70 – 100OverboughtStock has risen rapidly relative to recent history. Not a sell signal alone — in strong uptrends, RSI can stay overbought for weeks. Look for RSI divergence (price makes new high, RSI doesn't) as a warning.
40 – 60NeutralNo strong momentum bias. Price is consolidating or in a sideways range. Watch for a breakout in either direction.
0 – 30OversoldStock has fallen sharply. Potential reversal zone. Strongest buy signals occur when price touches the lower Bollinger Band AND RSI is below 30–35 simultaneously.
Divergence signals: Bullish divergence = price makes a new low but RSI makes a higher low (seller exhaustion). Bearish divergence = price makes a new high but RSI makes a lower high (buyer exhaustion). Both are strong reversal warnings.
🚀 Momentum Score

A composite 0–100 ranking of price momentum across multiple timeframes, normalized across all stocks in your watchlist so you can compare relative strength at a glance.

Momentum inputs:
  • Rate of Change (ROC) — 5, 10, 20 day price changes
  • EMA alignment — price position relative to EMA-12/26
  • Volume trend — rising vs declining volume confirmation
// Combined and normalized 0–100 across all watchlist stocks
  • ›80–100 — strongest momentum in your portfolio; trend is intact and accelerating
  • ›50–80 — moderate momentum; trend is present but not extreme
  • ›20–50 — weak or mixed momentum; stock may be consolidating or reversing
  • ›0–20 — weakest in the portfolio; strong downtrend. High caution for longs.
Bubble Chart use: Stocks in the upper-right quadrant of the Bubble Chart have both high momentum AND strong signals — your highest-conviction opportunities. Lower-left = weakest.
🎯 Convergence Score

The core SignalForge metric. Measures how many independent technical indicators all agree on the same directional view. Higher convergence = higher signal confidence.

Indicators checked:
  • Bollinger Band position (%B and bandwidth)
  • RSI (overbought / oversold level)
  • EMA-12/26 crossover direction
  • SuperTrend flip direction
  • Volume trend confirmation
  • Keltner squeeze status
  • MACD signal line crossover

Score 0–100. BUY fires at ≥65 (default). SELL fires at ≤35.
// Tune these thresholds in Settings → Signal Thresholds
Score RangeSignalMeaning
65 – 100BUYMajority of indicators agree: bullish. The higher the score, the stronger the conviction. 85+ = very high confidence setup.
36 – 64HOLDMixed signals. Indicators don't agree — staying neutral is prudent. Wait for a clearer setup.
0 – 35SELLMajority of indicators agree: bearish. Score near 0 = extremely weak, multiple confirming sell signals.
Tap "Criteria" on the Top Pick card to see the exact breakdown — which specific indicators are firing and contributing to the score for that stock right now.
📏 ATR — Average True Range

Measures the average daily price range, accounting for overnight gaps. ATR is not directional — it only measures volatility (how much the stock moves, not which way).

True Range = max of:
  • High − Low (intraday range)
  • |High − Previous Close| (gap up)
  • |Low − Previous Close| (gap down)

ATR(14) = Wilder's smoothed average of True Range over 14 days
// Used internally by SuperTrend and Keltner Channel calculations
  • ›Stop loss sizing: Place stops 1.5–2× ATR below entry to avoid being stopped out by normal daily noise.
  • ›Position sizing: Risk the same dollar amount per trade — allocate fewer shares in high-ATR stocks and more in low-ATR stocks.
  • ›ATR expansion — rising ATR means volatility is increasing; breakouts on expanding ATR are more reliable.

Analytics

Performance Stats Dashboard

Collapsible section showing detailed trading statistics. These metrics tell you not just whether you're profitable, but whether your system has edge and how efficiently it uses risk.

MetricFormulaWhat It Tells You
Win Rate %Winning trades ÷ Total closed trades × 100Percentage of closed trades that ended in profit. A 50% win rate is not inherently bad — what matters is the ratio of average winner to average loser (see Profit Factor). A system with 40% win rate and 3:1 reward/risk is extremely profitable.
Avg WinnerSum of profits ÷ Number of winning tradesAverage dollar or % gain on trades that closed profitably. Compare to Avg Loser. You want this to be significantly larger than your average loss.
Avg LoserSum of losses ÷ Number of losing tradesAverage dollar or % loss on trades that closed at a loss. If Avg Loser > Avg Winner, you need a very high win rate to be net profitable — a sign of a poorly sized stop strategy.
Profit FactorGross profits ÷ Gross lossesThe single best measure of a trading system's edge. >2.0 = strong edge. 1.25–2.0 = viable, worth refining. <1.0 = losing system. A Profit Factor of 1.5 means for every $1 lost, you make $1.50 — you keep $0.50 of net profit per dollar risked.
Max DrawdownLargest peak-to-trough equity decline during the periodYour worst-case loss scenario — how much your simulated account dropped from its high before recovering. If Max Drawdown is larger than you could emotionally tolerate in a real account, reduce position sizing. Target: keep drawdown under 20% of account.
Sharpe Ratio(Portfolio Return − Risk-Free Rate) ÷ Std Dev of ReturnsRisk-adjusted return. Measures how much return you earn per unit of volatility. >2.0 = excellent. 1.0–2.0 = good. <1.0 = too much risk for the return. A Sharpe of 1.5 means you're earning 1.5 units of return for every unit of risk taken.
Total / Closed TradesCount of all positions opened / count of exited positionsTotal includes active open positions. Closed is your completed track record. More closed trades = more statistical significance — 20+ closed trades gives you a meaningful read on your system's true edge.
Interpreting your stats together: A great system has Win Rate × Avg Winner > (1 − Win Rate) × Avg Loser, Profit Factor above 1.5, Max Drawdown under 15%, and Sharpe above 1.0. Use the ghost simulator to find that combination before risking real capital.

Strategy Cycling

How Cycling Works

The cycling engine tracks your strategy lifecycle automatically. Multi-leg cycles are grouped under a strategy header showing the cycle number and running income total.

// Example: Wheel strategy on AAPL
Cycle 1: Sell AAPL $190P Mar → expired worthless → +$320 premium
Cycle 2: Sell AAPL $195P Apr → assigned → +$280 premium (now own shares)
Cycle 3: Sell AAPL $200C May → called away → +$410 premium + stock gain
// Total income from AAPL wheel: $1,010 realized

When a trade expires or is closed, SignalForge logs the cycle and prompts you to start the next one — keeping the income machine running.
